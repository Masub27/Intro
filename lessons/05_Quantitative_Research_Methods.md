@section Introduction
Quant statistical methods. It aims to quantify relationships, test hypotheses, and generalize findings to larger populations.

@section Research Design
Research design outlines how a study will be conducted to answer specific questions. Common designs include experimental, quasi‑experimental, correlational, and cross‑sectional studies. Each design dictates the level of control, randomization, and the ability to infer causality.

@section Data Collection
Data collection involves selecting measurement instruments, sampling strategies, and procedures to gather reliable and valid data. Techniques include surveys, structured observations, standardized tests, and existing databases. Proper sampling (e.g., random, stratified) ensures representativeness.

@section Analysis
Analysis transforms raw data into meaningful results using descriptive statistics, inferential tests, and modeling. Techniques range from t‑tests and ANOVA to regression and factor analysis, depending on the research questions and data structure.

- question: What is the primary purpose of random sampling?
  type: mc
  options:
    - To ensure the sample size is large.
    - To eliminate all bias.
    - To make the sample representative of the population.
    - To simplify data analysis.
  answer: To make the sample representative of the population.

- question: Which statistical test is appropriate for comparing means of two independent groups?
  type: mc
  options:
